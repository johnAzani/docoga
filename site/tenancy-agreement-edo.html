What the law actually says in Edo

The statute: Rent Control and Recovery of Residential Premises Edict 1977 — Edict No. 4 of 1977, Bendel State of Nigeria — Cap. 74, Laws of Edo State. Commenced 1 March 1977.

The enactment still reads “Bendel State”: Bendel was split into Edo and Delta in 1991, and the Edict is received as Edo law. An academic paper cites it as “Cap R1”; the Judiciary’s own PDF carries “Cap. 74”. We use Cap. 74 and note the alternative.

Where a dispute goes

Edo is a tribunal state, not a magistrates’-court state. s.4 establishes Rent Tribunals constituted of (a) a chairman who has been in practice as a legal practitioner in Nigeria for not less than five years, and (b) two other persons of probity, appointed by the Governor. The chairman plus one other member is a quorum (s.4(4)), and the tribunal may sit with assessors where it thinks it necessary (s.4(6)).

s.41(2) repealed the Recovery of Premises Law to the extent that it relates to accommodation in residential premises, and s.41(3) repealed the Magistrates’ Court Law provisions on landlord-and-tenant actions over residential accommodation. s.2 defines “court” to include the High Court, magistrates’ courts and customary courts of the State.

Rent control

Edo operates a standard rent regime. s.5(1)–(2): it is unlawful for a landlord to accept an agreed rent above the standard rent, and where the agreed rent is higher the tenant pays the standard rent. s.5(3): where the agreed rent is lower, the tenant continues to pay it — but after six months the landlord may apply to the tribunal to vary it.

s.6(3): the standard rent fixed by the tribunal supersedes the agreed rent, and the order binds everyone including the landlord, the tenant and the mortgagee of the building.

Protection from retaliatory notices

Edo’s strongest tenant protection, and one of the strongest in the country. s.16(1): once a tenant has applied to the tribunal, any quit notice served afterwards is of no effect, and no similar notice may be given before the tribunal’s decision. s.16(2): the tribunal may declare a quit notice invalid and of no effect if it was not issued in good faith, and its endorsement to that effect on the notice is sufficient evidence of the decision before any court.

A tenant who takes a genuine dispute to the tribunal cannot be punished for it with a retaliatory quit notice.

A new tenant’s first six months

Where rent is payable in advance for a monthly or quarterly tenancy, the landlord may not give notice to quit or recover possession during the first six months of the tenancy — except on the order of the tribunal or with the tenant’s consent. A new Edo tenant who has paid in advance has a six-month statutory breathing space.

What your Edo State (Benin City) agreement should lock down

  • The notice period you actually want. Courts uphold what the agreement says — the statutory defaults apply only “where there is no stipulation”. If you want three months, write three months.
  • The rent and the advance — exact amount in figures and words, and how much is payable in advance.
  • The caution fee — amount, purpose, and the refund terms. This is the most disputed money in Nigerian tenancies.
  • The parties and the property — full names, addresses, and a clear description of the premises.
  • Covenants — repairs, utilities, subletting, use of the premises, and renewal terms.

Questions people ask

Is a written tenancy agreement compulsory in Edo State (Benin City)?

A tenancy can exist without one — but without it you have no agreed rent record, no caution-fee terms, and the statutory notice defaults govern. A two-minute written agreement is cheaper than any dispute.

Does the agreement override the notice periods in the law?

Generally yes — the statutory periods apply where the agreement is silent. Write the notice you want into the agreement.

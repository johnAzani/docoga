What the law actually says in Lagos

The statute: Tenancy Law of Lagos State 2011 — Law No. 14 of 2011, Laws of Lagos State. Commenced Assented at Ikeja 24 August 2011; in force 24 August 2011 (s.49).

Where a dispute goes

There is no Rent Tribunal in Lagos. The tribunals were abolished and their functions transferred to the Magistrates’ Courts, and s.48 of the 2011 Law repealed the Rent Tribunals (Abolition and Transfer of Functions) Law 2007. s.47 defines “Court” as the High Court and Magistrates’ Court of Lagos State and expressly excludes the Customary Court. Under s.2(4) proceedings go to the High Court where the rental value exceeds the Magistrates’ Court jurisdiction under the Magistrates’ Courts Law.

s.2(3): agreeing to use the Lagos Multi-Door Courthouse or a Citizens Mediation Centre does not oust the Court’s jurisdiction. s.32: the Court must promote reconciliation and may refer a case to mediation without the parties’ consent; the mediator files the agreement for endorsement within 3 days.

The 2011 Law does not use the words “caution fee”. What it regulates is the money taken alongside rent: a security deposit, charges for services and facilities, and service charges (s.10(a)–(c)). For each, the landlord owes the tenant a separate receipt and a six-monthly written account. Separately, s.11 provides that the party who engages a professional pays that professional’s fee — so the practice of the tenant funding the landlord’s lawyer’s fee is not supported by the Law.

A bill before the Lagos State House of Assembly would go further and regulate agents’ commissions. It has not been passed and must not be described as current law.

Who repairs what

s.7(3): the tenant keeps the premises in good and tenantable repair, reasonable wear and tear excepted. s.8(vi): the landlord effects repairs and maintains the external and common parts. s.8 also requires the landlord to keep the premises insured, not to disturb quiet enjoyment, to pay all rates and charges stipulated by law, not to withdraw a common facility or service, and not to seize any item or property of the tenant or interfere with the tenant’s access to it — which is the statutory answer to lock-outs and seized belongings.

Where this law does not apply

The 2011 Law does not apply in four areas: Apapa, Ikeja GRA, Ikoyi and Victoria Island. s.1(3) also lets the Governor exempt further areas by Order published in the State Official Gazette. Practitioners apply the older Rent Control and Recovery of Residential Premises Law 1997 and/or the Recovery of Premises Law, Cap. 118 (1973) in these areas instead — which means the advance-rent cap in s.4 does not protect a tenant in Ikoyi the way it protects one in Yaba.

  • residential premises owned or operated by an educational institution for its staff and students (s.1(2)(a))
  • residential premises provided for emergency shelter (s.1(2)(b))
  • residential premises in a care or hospice facility, or in a public or private hospital or mental health facility (s.1(2)(c))
  • premises made available in the course of rehabilitative or therapeutic treatment (s.1(2)(d))

Other protections worth knowing

  • s.6 — quiet and peaceable enjoyment includes privacy, freedom from unreasonable disturbance, exclusive possession (subject to the landlord’s restricted right of inspection) and use of common areas.
  • s.6(2) — a tenant who made improvements with the landlord’s previous written consent may claim compensation for them on quitting.
  • s.29 — where a landlord refuses to accept rent, the tenant may apply to the Court to pay the rent into the Court Registry.
  • s.44(1) — demolishing, altering or modifying a building to eject a tenant without the Court’s approval, forcibly ejecting or attempting to, or threatening or molesting a tenant, is an offence: ₦250,000 or 6 months’ imprisonment.
  • s.41 — a warrant for possession runs for three months and may be renewed every three months, but not more than three renewals.
  • s.42 — no entry on a warrant may be made on a Sunday or public holiday.

Protecting your caution fee in Lagos

  • Receipt the deposit separately from rent — never lump it into the rent figure.
  • Refund terms in writing: refundable at tenancy’s end, fair wear and tear excepted.
  • Itemised deductions only — any deduction must be listed in writing with evidence.
  • Record the property’s condition at move-in (photos, a short checklist) — it settles most arguments before they start.
  • Timeline: state when the refund is due after the tenancy ends.

Questions people ask

Is a caution fee the same as rent?

No — it is a refundable security deposit held against damage or unpaid charges. It is not the landlord’s income.

How much can a caution fee be?

Commonly a fraction of the rent, but it is negotiable — record whatever you agree in the tenancy agreement and receipt it.

What if the landlord refuses to refund?

Your receipt with written refund terms, plus your move-in condition record, is exactly what the Magistrates’ Courts and the High Court of Lagos State will want to see.

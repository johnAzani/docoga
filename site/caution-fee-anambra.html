What the law actually says in Anambra

The statute: Landlord and Tenant Law — Cap. 76, Laws of Anambra State. Commenced date not stated.

Identified by the Court of Appeal as the law governing recovery of possession of premises in Anambra State, and applied in the state from which Enugu was carved in 1991.

Where a dispute goes

s.144 of the Landlord and Tenant Law follows the Abuja pattern: it discourages commencing an action in the High Court where the matter is one the Magistrate Court can entertain.

Wigwe notes the constitutional limit on that: a provision purporting wholly to exclude the High Court’s jurisdiction would be inconsistent with s.272 of the 1999 Constitution (as amended). So the High Court retains jurisdiction in Anambra; s.144 channels the ordinary case downward, it does not shut the High Court out.

We have not read Cap. 76 on premiums, caution fees or deposits, so no Anambra rule is stated. Note that neighbouring Edo (s.6(4)), Cross River (s.6(4)) and Kwara (s.5(4)) all ban premiums outright — but those are their statutes, not Anambra’s, and must not be attributed here.

A correction to our own research

A correction to our own earlier research.

We previously recorded Anambra as having no tenancy legislation in force. That was wrong. The Court of Appeal in Amah v. Ozouli [2010] NGCA 6 (CA/E/298/2006) identifies the Landlord and Tenant Law, Cap. 76, Laws of Anambra State as the law governing recovery of possession, and quotes from it. What is pending in Anambra is a new Rent and Tenancy Law — not the first one.

Alternative accommodation

Anambra is the second jurisdiction we have found, after Kwara, where the court must be satisfied about alternative accommodation. On the Court of Appeal’s quotation of the law: no order for recovery of possession or ejectment may be made unless the court is satisfied that suitable alternative accommodation is available for the tenant.

s.173(2): where the court considers it reasonable to do so, it may make the order without proof of suitable alternative accommodation if one or more of the circumstances set out in s.174 are proved to its satisfaction.

This is the same architecture as Kwara s.26 and its Second Schedule, and the Eastern/Northern cousin of the Cross River Schedule 3 list. Edo, by contrast, lets the tribunal dispense with proof of alternative accommodation wherever it considers it reasonable. The full text of s.174 has not been read, so the individual circumstances are not listed here.

A related jurisdiction

Enugu inherited this law. The Court of Appeal in Amah v. Ozouli held that the law applicable in Enugu State is the Landlord and Tenant Law, Cap. 101, Revised Laws of Enugu State, which came into operation on 27 August 1991 — resolving the earlier conflict in our notes between “Cap. 101 of 2004” and other dates. The 1991 date is when it commenced; 2004 is the revised edition.

Other protections worth knowing

  • A landlord may not resort to self-help. Unlawful eviction is actionable in damages.
  • The High Court retains jurisdiction in Anambra notwithstanding s.144 — a provision wholly excluding it would offend s.272 of the 1999 Constitution.
  • Improvements made with the landlord’s written consent may found a counterclaim for their unexhausted value.

What we could not confirm

We publish the gaps rather than fill them in:

  • The full text of Cap. 76 — not read. Section contents are known only from the Court of Appeal’s quotation of ss.172–174.
  • The individual circumstances listed in s.174 (possession without proof of alternative accommodation).
  • Whether Anambra has a separate rent-control or recovery-of-premises statute alongside the Landlord and Tenant Law.
  • Whether the proposed Rent and Tenancy Law has been passed — treat as pending until confirmed.

Protecting your caution fee in Anambra State (Onitsha / Awka)

  • Receipt the deposit separately from rent — never lump it into the rent figure.
  • Refund terms in writing: refundable at tenancy’s end, fair wear and tear excepted.
  • Itemised deductions only — any deduction must be listed in writing with evidence.
  • Record the property’s condition at move-in (photos, a short checklist) — it settles most arguments before they start.
  • Timeline: state when the refund is due after the tenancy ends.

Questions people ask

Is a caution fee the same as rent?

No — it is a refundable security deposit held against damage or unpaid charges. It is not the landlord’s income.

How much can a caution fee be?

Commonly a fraction of the rent, but it is negotiable — record whatever you agree in the tenancy agreement and receipt it.

What if the landlord refuses to refund?

Your receipt with written refund terms, plus your move-in condition record, is exactly what the Magistrates’ Courts and the High Court of Anambra State will want to see.

The Hook

My people, we know say rent for Nigeria, especially for places like Lagos and Abuja, dey vex person. Every year, landlords dey increase rent, and e fit feel like say nothing dey work in your favour. But I get good news for you today! There's a new federal law that can help ease that burden a bit. Imagine say the money you pay for rent can actually reduce the tax you pay to government. Yes, you heard me right! From January 1, 2026, a new provision in the Nigeria Tax Act is here to give tenants like you some financial breathing room. This is not just talk, it is the law of the land. This new rent relief means you don't just pay rent; you can use it to your advantage. Make we dive in!

What is Rent Relief?

So, wetin be this rent relief exactly? Simply put, the Nigeria Tax Act of 2025, which started running from January 1, 2026, now allows you, as an individual taxpayer, to deduct a portion of your annual rent when calculating your taxable income. This means a smaller part of your earnings will be taxed. Specifically, Section 30(2)(a)(vi) of the Act says you can get a rent relief of 20% of the annual rent you paid. This relief is not unlimited though; it comes with a maximum cap of five hundred thousand naira. This new provision replaces the old Consolidated Relief Allowance from the Personal Income Tax Act. So, if you pay rent, you now have a new way to save on your taxes, but if you don't pay rent, then you won't get this rent relief.

How Much Can You Save?

Let's break down how much you can save with practical figures. The relief is 20% of your annual rent, up to a maximum of ₦500,000. For example, if your annual rent is one million, two hundred thousand naira, ₦1,200,000, you get a relief of ₦240,000. If your rent is one million, five hundred thousand naira, ₦1,500,000, your relief is ₦300,000. For any rent from two million, five hundred thousand naira, ₦2,500,000, or more, you get the maximum relief of ₦500,000. This relief reduces your taxable income, not your tax bill directly. The actual naira saved on tax depends on your personal income tax rate, as outlined in the Fourth Schedule of the Nigeria Tax Act 2025.

Who Can Claim It and How?

This rent relief is specifically for individuals, not companies. It applies to you if you are an employee under the PAYE system, or if you are self-employed. But listen carefully, this relief is claimed, not automatically given. If you don't declare your rent, your deduction is zero. For employees, you declare to your employer, who then applies it through your PAYE deductions from the month you submit your evidence. If you are self-employed or not under PAYE, you claim it in your annual tax return to your State tax authority. For FCT residents and others assessed by the Nigeria Revenue Service, you claim it with them. This is a crucial step to open up your savings.

The Documents You MUST Have

To claim this relief, proper documentation is key. The Joint Revenue Board PIT Guidelines 2026, paragraph 9.6, spells out the minimum information required. You need your legal tenant's name, the actual rent paid and the period it covers, your landlord's full name along with their mobile number, Tax ID or NIN, and the property address. A proper rent receipt in your own name, showing all these details, plus your signed tenancy agreement and proof of payment, is what HR departments and tax offices will ask for. Without these records, you cannot claim the relief. Paper beats memory, my friend, especially with tax matters.

Important Dates and Deadlines

Timing is important for your tax claims. For the 2026 tax year, employers need to submit their annual PAYE returns by January 31, 2027. For individuals, whether self-employed or claiming outside PAYE, your annual return for 2026 is due by March 31, 2027. What if your rent payment straddles two different years? The law has a provision for that. Rent that covers periods in two different tax years will be apportioned to each year. For example, if you pay two million naira for 24 months, one million naira will count for 2026, giving you a relief of two hundred thousand naira for that year. So, track your rent payments carefully.

Shared Tenancies and Other Rules

What about shared tenancies? The Joint Revenue Board PIT Guidelines 2026, in paragraphs 9.3 to 9.5, clarifies this. In a shared tenancy, each tenant can claim up to the ₦500,000 cap, but only on the portion of rent they personally bear. So, if you share a flat and split the rent, you claim relief on your part. The relief is also on an actual-year basis, meaning rent paid in 2026 relieves your 2026 taxes. This is a federal law, applying nationwide. While states like Lagos and Anambra have their own tenancy laws for things like notice periods or advance rent, this rent relief is a federal provision, uniform across Nigeria.

Why This Matters to You, The Tenant

This new rent relief is a big deal for tenants across Nigeria. It's a clear demonstration that government recognizes the burden of rent and is providing a tangible way to ease it. In a time where reported rents in places like Lagos have risen 25-40%, every kobo saved counts. This law empowers you. It turns your rent payment into a tax advantage, giving you a legitimate path to reduce your taxable income. But remember, the power is in your hands to declare and document properly. Without that, this benefit remains just a good intention. Get your papers right, and claim what is rightfully yours.

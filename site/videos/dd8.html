The Hook

Picture a tenant in Port Harcourt coming home on a rainy Tuesday to find a typed paper taped to the iron gate. The landlord writes that rent finished yesterday, so the tenant must pack out in seven days. Across town in Lagos, another family gets a six-month notice handed to them in August, telling them to vacate by next February. Both families panic, pack their bags in a rush, and begin begging agents. But here is the truth of Nigerian property law. Expiry of rent does not mean you have lost your home. In Rivers, the expiry of rent does not end the tenancy, and a tenancy is determined by a valid notice or effluxion of a fixed term. A landlord who acts as if running out of rent automatically ejects you is acting unlawfully. Before anyone packs a single box, you must check the date, check the law, and check the calendar.

The Rivers Anniversary Rule

If your rented house is anywhere in Rivers State, from Old GRA to Choba, you must understand the strict anniversary rule. In Rivers State under section 7 of the Recovery of Premises Law, a notice to quit must terminate on the eve of the anniversary of the current term. That means the notice must end on the exact day before the next rental cycle begins. It cannot end whenever the landlord feels angry, and it cannot terminate in the middle of a term. For a yearly tenancy in Rivers that began on 1 January 2025, a valid six-month quit notice must expire on 31 December 2025, which is the eve of the anniversary. If the landlord serves a notice that terminates a week earlier or two weeks later, the date is wrong. Get the date wrong and that notice is completely dead on arrival.

The Lagos Exception

Why do so many landlords and agents get this confused? Because they copy practices from Lagos and bring them down to Port Harcourt. Section 13 subsection 4 of the Lagos Tenancy Law 2011 is the exception, where three- and six-month notices need not end on the anniversary and may end on or after the date the tenancy expires. In Lagos, the landlord can let the six months run past the original renewal date. But Rivers does not follow that Lagos shortcut. In Rivers and most other states, the eve-of-anniversary rule is mandatory. Furthermore, in Lagos under section 13 subsection 5, a fixed-term tenancy needs no quit notice at all once it determines by effluxion of time, requiring only a seven-day notice of intention to recover possession in Form TL5. What works in Ikeja will fail in a Port Harcourt court.

The Statutory Notice Ladder

How much time does the law actually require a landlord to give? In Rivers under section 6 subsection 1 d, a yearly tenant is entitled to six months notice. If you pay your rent annually, no landlord can wake up and give you one month or three months. Under section 6 subsection 1 a in Rivers, a tenant at will or weekly tenant receives one week notice. Many landlords try to change this by calling a yearly tenant a weekly caretaker in a spoken argument. But under section 6 subsection 2 in Rivers, the nature of a tenancy is determined, absent contrary evidence, by reference to the mode of payment and demand for rent. If you pay once a year, the court views you as a yearly tenant entitled to your full six months notice ending on the proper anniversary eve.

A Process of Court, Not a Letter

Many landlords believe they can just scribble a quit notice on plain paper or hire an estate agent to write a harsh warning letter. That is an expensive mistake. The Court of Appeal in Anyaehie v Ongolo held on sections 5 to 7 of the Rivers Law that a notice to quit is a process of court addressed to a known tenant and not a letter. It is not an informal memo. Under section 5 in Rivers, a notice to quit is given in Form B when signed by the landlord himself, Form C when signed by an agent or solicitor, and the seven-day notice under section 8 is Form E. If the landlord hands you a regular letter from his office without the required statutory form, that document is defective. In Rivers, a defect in the issue, content or service of a notice vitiates the whole proceeding.

The Seven Clear Days Rule

What happens after that six-month quit notice runs its full course? The landlord still cannot touch your door lock. After the quit notice expires in Rivers, the landlord must serve a further written notice of the owner intention to apply to court to recover possession of not less than seven clear days. This seven-day notice cannot be issued while the quit notice is still running. It can only be served after the quit notice has completely expired. Even then, the calculation of time is strict. For the seven clear days notice, the day of service is excluded and the day of expiry included. The date of service is the material date, not the date written on the notice. If the bailiff serves it on Monday, you do not count Monday. Rushing the count renders the action invalid.

Who Counts as a Tenant

Landlords often claim that relatives, domestic assistants, or caretakers living on the property have no rights at all and can be kicked onto the street at midnight. The law says otherwise. Under section 29 in Rivers, a tenant includes any person occupying premises whether on payment of rent or otherwise, but not a person occupying under a bona fide claim of ownership. Even if you pay zero naira, or render service instead of cash, you are legally a tenant. Moreover, under section 75 subsection 1 of the Landlord and Tenant Law in Rivers, rent is payable in arrears unless the parties agree otherwise. Paying upfront is actually an agreed concession. Every lawful occupant is entitled to formal statutory service before any legal step can be taken to recover possession.

No Self-Help Allowed

When landlords realize their notices are defective, some resort to self-help tactics like removing roofs, cutting off electricity, or locking gates with heavy chains. Under sections 20 to 22 in Rivers, an occupier whose tenancy has ended can be put out only in pursuance of a court order obtained after the Law notices. A landlord who takes the law into his own hands commits an actionable wrong that sounds in damages. And in Lagos under section 44 subsection 1 of the Lagos Tenancy Law 2011, demolishing, altering or modifying a building to eject a tenant without court approval is an offence punishable by 250,000 naira fine or 6 months imprisonment. The law does not reward street violence. A tenant who knows the sections can resist intimidation calmly with the text of the statute.

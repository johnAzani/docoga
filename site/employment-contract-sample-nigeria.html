The sample (a small business hiring a sales assistant, Lagos)

EMPLOYMENT CONTRACT

Private & Confidential

BETWEEN: [EMPLOYER'S BUSINESS NAME], of [business address, Lagos] (“the
Employer”) of the one part;

AND: [EMPLOYEE'S FULL NAME], of [employee's address] (“the Employee”) of the
other part.

1. POSITION AND DUTIES. The Employer hereby employs the Employee as [Sales
Assistant], and the Employee accepts such employment. The Employee shall:
[attend to customers, keep the shop floor stocked and tidy, record each day's
sales and bank the takings], and shall perform such other duties as the
Employer may reasonably assign.

2. COMMENCEMENT AND PROBATION. Employment commences on [1 December 2026]. The
first [3] months constitute a period of probation, during which either party
may terminate this contract with [one week's] written notice. Confirmation of
employment will be communicated in writing on satisfactory completion of
probation.

3. REMUNERATION. The Employer shall pay the Employee a salary of [One Hundred
and Twenty Thousand (₦120,000) Naira Only] per month, payable not later than
the 28th day of each month, subject to statutory deductions (including PAYE
tax and the Employee's pension contribution where applicable).

4. HOURS AND PLACE OF WORK. The normal working hours shall be [Monday to
Saturday, 8:00am to 5:00pm, with one hour's break]. The principal place of
work shall be [the shop at 12 Example Street, Surulere, Lagos], and the
Employee may be required to work at such other place as the Employer's
business reasonably requires.

5. ANNUAL LEAVE AND SICK LEAVE. The Employee shall be entitled to [15] working
days of annual leave for each completed year of service, to be taken at times
approved by the Employer. The Employee shall further be entitled to paid sick
leave of up to twelve working days in any calendar year, on the certificate of
a registered medical practitioner.

6. CONFIDENTIALITY. The Employee shall not, during or after the termination of
this employment, disclose to any person any confidential information relating
to the business, affairs, customers or finances of the Employer, except as
required by law.

7. TERMINATION AND NOTICE. After probation, either party may terminate this
employment by giving [one month's] written notice (or payment in lieu). The
Employer may terminate summarily for gross misconduct. All Employer property
shall be returned on the last day of employment.

8. GOVERNING LAW. This contract shall be governed by the laws of the Federal
Republic of Nigeria.

IN WITNESS WHEREOF the parties have executed this contract on the date first
written above.


______________________________
For: [Employer's business name] — Employer — signature & date

______________________________
[Employee's full name] — Employee — signature & date

The floors behind the brackets (Labour Act, Cap L1 LFN 2004, for a 'worker' as
s.91 defines one): a written statement of terms within three months of
engagement (s.7); notice after probation of at least one day, one week, two
weeks or one month by length of service (s.11(2)) — the contract's one month
is above that floor; wages at intervals of not more than one month (s.15);
paid sick leave of up to twelve working days a year (s.16); at least six
working days' holiday after twelve months (s.18). The salary shown is above
the national minimum wage of ₦70,000 a month (National Minimum Wage
(Amendment) Act 2024).

What each clause is for

  • 1. Position and duties. The job title and a short list of what the job is. Write the list: a dispute about “that is not my work” is settled by this clause, and the Act requires the nature of the employment to be in writing (s.7(1)(c)).
  • 2. Commencement and probation. The start date fixes everything else — the notice ladder, the twelve months to the first holiday, the three months for the written statement. Probation is not in the Act; it is a contractual period with a shorter notice, and it must be confirmed in writing when it ends or it simply runs on.
  • 3. Remuneration. The salary in figures and words, the pay day, and the deductions — PAYE and, where the pension scheme applies, the employee’s 8%. A pay period longer than a month needs the State Authority’s consent (s.9(4)); the 28th of each month is inside the rule.
  • 4. Hours and place of work. The Act leaves normal hours to the agreement (s.13(1)), so the days and times in this clause are the rule for this job. The place of work is where the worker must report; the “other place” sentence covers a second branch without a new contract.
  • 5. Annual leave and sick leave. Fifteen working days is the common private-sector figure and sits above the Act’s six (s.18). The sick-leave sentence is the one most templates omit — s.7(1)(g) requires the terms on sickness and sick pay to be stated, and s.16 sets the twelve-day floor.
  • 6. Confidentiality. Customer lists, prices, suppliers, takings. The clause survives the end of the employment; it is not a non-compete, which Nigerian courts read narrowly.
  • 7. Termination and notice. One month each way after probation — longer than any step of the s.11 ladder, so lawful for every length of service — with payment in lieu allowed (s.11(6)). Summary dismissal is reserved for gross misconduct; a poor sales month is not gross misconduct.
  • 8. Governing law. Nigerian law, which means the Labour Act, the National Industrial Court for disputes, and the state’s PAYE authority.

The Labour Act floors, clause by clause

Term in the contractThe statutory floor (Labour Act, Cap L1 LFN 2004)Section
Written termsWithin three months of engagement the employer gives the worker a written statement of: the employer’s name; the worker’s name and address and the place and date of engagement; the nature of the employment; the expiry date of a fixed-term contract; the notice period; wages and how and how often they are paid; hours of work; holidays and holiday pay; sickness and sick pay; and any special conditions. A signed contract that carries all of these is that statement.s.7(1)
AgeNo one under 16 may be employed under a contract of this kind.s.9(3)
NoticeEither side may end the contract on notice of at least one day (up to three months’ service), one week (over three months, under two years), two weeks (two to five years) or one month (five years and more). Notice of a week or more must be in writing; either side may pay wages in lieu instead. The contract may give longer notice — never shorter.s.11(2), (3), (6)
HoursNormal hours are fixed by the agreement, by collective bargaining or by a wages board — the Act does not itself set a weekly maximum, so the hours clause is the whole of the rule.s.13(1)
Pay dayWages are due at the end of each pay period, and a pay period may not be longer than one month.s.15
Sick leaveWages are payable for up to twelve working days of absence in a calendar year, on a registered medical practitioner’s certificate.s.16
Annual leaveAfter twelve months’ continuous service, at least six working days of holiday with full pay (twelve for a worker under sixteen). The holiday may not be bought out with cash while the contract runs.s.18(1), (3)
Who the floors protectThe Act’s “worker” excludes staff in administrative, executive, technical or professional roles, members of the employer’s family and agents who work outside the employer’s premises on commission. For them the contract governs — which is why it should be written, and written clearly.s.91(1)

Outside the Labour Act: the national minimum wage is ₦70,000 a month (National Minimum Wage (Amendment) Act 2024; the Minimum Wage Act exempts establishments employing fewer than 25 people and part-time or commission work); PAYE is deducted from each salary and remitted to the tax authority of the state where the employee lives; where the Pension Reform Act 2014 applies, the employer contributes at least 10% and the employee 8% of monthly emoluments.

Before the contract is signed — the employer’s checklist

  • Two copies, both signed, one for each side. The Act wants the worker to have the written terms within three months (s.7(1)); handing over a signed copy on day one is the simplest proof.
  • Check the age. Nobody under sixteen under a contract of this kind (s.9(3)); keep a copy of the ID you saw.
  • Register for PAYE with the tax authority of the state where the employee lives, and keep the payslips — the deductions clause is only lawful if the money actually reaches the authority.
  • Decide the pension question early. Under the Pension Reform Act 2014 the employer’s share is at least 10% of monthly emoluments on top of salary; a salary quoted without it is a salary that will be renegotiated.
  • Keep the leave record. Holiday may not be paid out in cash while the contract runs (s.18(3)), so an untaken year carries forward in the record rather than disappearing.

If you are the employee signing it

  • Count the notice both ways. The notice you must give is the notice you are owed; a contract that asks you for a month and gives you a week is lopsided, and for a Labour Act worker the employer’s week is only lawful for service under two years (s.11(2)).
  • Look for the leave and sick-leave sentences. If they are missing, ask for them in writing before signing — the Act requires them to be stated (s.7(1)(g)).
  • Deductions must be named. PAYE and pension are lawful; “shortages”, “breakages” and unexplained fines are not deductions a contract can authorise in advance.
  • Keep your copy. At the National Industrial Court, the signed contract is the first exhibit — for both sides.

Questions people ask

How do I write an employment contract in Nigeria?

Name the employer and the employee; state the position and duties, the start date and any probation, the salary in figures and words with the pay day and deductions, the hours and place of work, annual leave and sick leave, confidentiality, the notice each side must give, and the governing law; both sign, each keeps a copy. The sample above is that contract; the generator fills it in.

Is a written contract compulsory?

For a worker the Labour Act covers, the employer must give a written statement of the main terms within three months of the start (s.7(1)). A signed contract that carries all of those terms satisfies the section. For staff the Act’s definition of “worker” excludes — administrative, executive, technical and professional roles — the contract is not required by the Act, but it is the only thing that then governs the job.

What is the minimum notice period in Nigeria?

Under s.11(2) of the Labour Act: one day for service up to three months, one week for over three months but under two years, two weeks for two to five years, and one month for five years or more — and notice of a week or more must be written. A contract may give longer notice; this sample gives one month either way after probation.

How many days of annual leave does the law give?

At least six working days with full pay after twelve months’ continuous service (s.18(1)); most private employers give more, and the sample gives fifteen. Sick leave is separate: up to twelve working days a year with a medical certificate (s.16).

Can I put a three-month probation in the contract?

Yes. Probation is a contractual term, not a statutory one; the sample uses three months with one week’s notice during it. For a Labour Act worker in the first three months the statutory floor is one day, so a week is above it. Confirm the employee in writing when probation ends.

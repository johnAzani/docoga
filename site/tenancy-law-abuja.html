Tenancy Law in Abuja: Notice Periods, Rent, Courts

Which law governs a tenancy in Abuja, how many months’ notice it requires, what it says about receipts, advance rent and caution money, and which court or tribunal hears a dispute. Every rule below carries its section; where we could not verify a point from the primary text we say so instead of guessing.

Notice-to-quit minimums in Abuja

Verified against Recovery of Premises Act — Cap. 544, Laws of the Federation of Nigeria (Abuja) 1990 — also cited as LFN 2004. Where the agreement is silent, the minimum notice is:

Type of tenancyMinimum notice to quit
Tenant at will or weekly tenancyone week
Monthly tenancyone month — and it must be one calendar month
Quarterly tenancya quarter (three months)
Yearly tenancyhalf a year (six calendar months)

Source: Recovery of Premises Act, Cap. 544, Laws of the Federation of Nigeria (Abuja) 1990 — also cited as LFN 2004.

This is a genuine gap in the law, and we say so. The Recovery of Premises Act sets no limit on how much rent a landlord may demand in advance in the FCT, and attaches no penalty to demanding more than a year. Unlike a Lagos tenant, an Abuja tenant asked for two or three years’ rent up front has no statutory cap to point to. Whatever is agreed should be recorded in the tenancy agreement before money moves.

No arrears shortcut. Lagos s.13(2)–(3) and the Cross River and Kwara provisos let a tenancy lapse on defined arrears with no quit notice. The FCT Act has no equivalent — serve the notice.

What the law actually says in Abuja (FCT)

The statute: Recovery of Premises Act — Cap. 544, Laws of the Federation of Nigeria (Abuja) 1990 — also cited as LFN 2004. Commenced 1990.

Although an Act of the National Assembly, it is not a federal law of general application. It was made specially for the Federal Capital Territory. There is no federal landlord-and-tenant statute for Nigeria as a whole.

Where a dispute goes

Recovery follows the Act through the FCT courts — Magistrates’/District Courts and the FCT High Court. One practitioner source puts the line at an annual rental value of ₦5,000,000 — District Court at or below, FCT High Court above. This figure rests on a single source and is shown on the page as such (tier C).

Other protections worth knowing

  • A landlord may not resort to self-help. Once the seven days expire the occupier becomes a statutory tenant, removable only by a lawful court order.
  • s.20 — the Court may issue a warrant for possession even where a counterclaim is undetermined or unsatisfied.
  • A landlord who obtains an order for possession by intentionally providing false information commits an offence, and the Court may order reasonable compensation to the tenant.

Questions people ask

Is there a tenancy law in Abuja?

The block below names the statute we verified for Abuja with its citation and sections — or, where we could not cite one from a source we trust, says so and sets out the general Nigerian position that applies instead.

How many months’ notice does a landlord have to give in Abuja?

Where the agreement is silent: one week for a tenant at will, one month for a monthly tenant, three months for a quarterly tenant and six months for a yearly tenant — the table above shows the Abuja ladder with its section. A written agreement can fix its own period.

Can my landlord throw me out without going to court in Abuja?

No. Across Nigeria possession is recovered through the District Courts and the High Court of the FCT, Abuja after the notice to quit and the 7 days’ notice have expired. Lock-outs, removing doors or roofs and seizing belongings are self-help the courts do not allow.

Does the agreement override the law in Abuja?

On notice periods, generally yes — the statutory periods apply where the agreement is silent. Protections written as offences or prohibitions (for example a receipt duty or an advance-rent cap) cannot be contracted out of.

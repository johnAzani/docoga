Notice-to-quit minimums in Kano State

We could not verify this state’s own statute, so the table below is the general Nigerian position rather than a citation:

Type of tenancyMinimum notice to quit
Tenant at will1 week
Monthly1 month
Quarterly3 months (a quarter)
Yearly6 months (half a year)

This is the general Nigerian position. We have not yet verified Kano State’s own tenancy statute — see the note below.

No Kano advance-rent cap has been found, and no rent-control statute to put one in. On the present state of the sources there is no statutory limit on what a Kano landlord may ask for in advance, and no penalty for asking. Whatever is agreed must therefore go into the tenancy agreement before money moves — that agreement is the only protection available.

The statutory periods are minimums; longer is valid, shorter by a day is not.

What the law actually says in Kano

No citable statute found. We could not locate a tenancy or rent-control statute for Kano from any source we trust. Rather than name one we cannot cite, the sections below set out what we did find.

What we found when we looked

We looked properly, twice, and found nothing. That is the finding.

That absence is itself the useful information. A Kano tenant has no statutory figure to point to and no tribunal to apply to about the rent itself — so everything turns on the tenancy agreement. It also means any page or agent quoting a “Kano rent control law” is inventing it.

Two separate research passes for a Kano rent-control or tenancy statute returned no Nigerian authority at all — the results were Kenyan, Indian and United States landlord-and-tenant law. On the present state of published sources there is no Kano rent-control statute we can cite, which means there is no Kano standard-rent regime, no Kano rent tribunal we can identify, and no Kano advance-rent cap.

What probably governs recovery of possession instead

Kano was created out of the former Northern Region. The peer-reviewed account of how Nigerian tenancy law travelled is that the regions each reproduced the original Recovery of Premises Ordinance, and that on the creation of twelve states in 1968 each new state inherited all the existing laws of the region it was carved from — later states inheriting in turn, mutatis mutandis, under the States Creation and Transitional Provisions Act.

This is inference, not confirmation. It rests on the constitutional framework for the succession of regional laws, not on a Kano statute or a Kano judgment we have read. We publish it as the most likely starting point and say plainly that it is unconfirmed. Do not rely on it without local advice.

The Northern Region’s recovery law is cited as the Recovery of Premises Law, Laws of Northern Nigeria 1963 — given as Cap. 113, Vol. 3 in one source and as Cap. 115 in another. Those citations conflict and we have not resolved them.

Other protections worth knowing

  • A landlord may not resort to self-help; unlawful eviction is actionable in damages.
  • The High Court has jurisdiction to entertain recovery proceedings in every state, whatever the lower-court position.
  • Improvements made with the landlord’s written consent may found a counterclaim for their unexhausted value.

Questions people ask

Is there a tenancy law in Kano State?

The block below names the statute we verified for Kano State with its citation and sections — or, where we could not cite one from a source we trust, says so and sets out the general Nigerian position that applies instead.

How many months’ notice does a landlord have to give in Kano State?

Where the agreement is silent: one week for a tenant at will, one month for a monthly tenant, three months for a quarterly tenant and six months for a yearly tenant — the table above shows the Kano State ladder with its section. A written agreement can fix its own period.

Can my landlord throw me out without going to court in Kano State?

No. Across Nigeria possession is recovered through the courts of Kano (not yet confirmed from the primary text) after the notice to quit and the 7 days’ notice have expired. Lock-outs, removing doors or roofs and seizing belongings are self-help the courts do not allow.

Does the agreement override the law in Kano State?

On notice periods, generally yes — the statutory periods apply where the agreement is silent. Protections written as offences or prohibitions (for example a receipt duty or an advance-rent cap) cannot be contracted out of.

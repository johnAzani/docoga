How the two statutory lines were worked out. Pension: 8% of basic + housing +
transport (₦120,000) = ₦9,600 (Pension Reform Act 2014, s.4(1)(b); the
employer adds at least 10% — ₦12,000 — on top, never from the employee). PAYE:
annual gross ₦1,440,000 less the year's pension ₦115,200 = chargeable
₦1,324,800; the first ₦800,000 at 0%, the remaining ₦524,800 at 15% = ₦78,720
a year = ₦6,560 a month (Nigeria Tax Act 2025, Fourth Schedule; no rent relief
declared). An employee earning the national minimum wage of ₦70,000 or less
pays no PAYE at all (NTA 2025, s.163). NHF is 2.5% of monthly income but,
since the Business Facilitation Act 2023 (s.45), only if a private-sector
employee chooses to contribute. Total deductions of any kind may not exceed
one-third of the month's wages (Labour Act, s.5(7)).

What each line is for

  • Employer, employee, period, pay date. The payslip is evidence — of employment, of income, of what was deducted. A bank or an embassy reads these four lines first; a payslip with no pay period proves nothing.
  • Basic, housing, transport. The three components the pension law reads (the 8% and the 10% are calculated on at least their sum), which is why Nigerian salaries are split this way even when the employee never sees a difference.
  • Gross pay. The whole of the month’s earnings before anything is taken out — the figure a lender multiplies.
  • PAYE. The employee’s income tax, withheld by the employer and paid to the state where the employee lives. Under the 2026 bands a worker on ₦120,000 a month pays about ₦6,560; a worker on ₦70,000 pays nothing.
  • Pension, NHF, other deductions. Each named, each with its rule in the table below. A line called “deductions” with no breakdown is the first thing to query.
  • Net pay, in figures and in words. What reached the account. The words are there for the same reason they are on a cheque — a figure can be altered; a sentence cannot be altered quietly.
  • “Computer-generated, no signature required.” Standard, and lawful; what gives the document weight is that it matches the bank transfer and the employer’s records.

The worked example, step by step

  • Monthly: basic ₦70,000 + housing ₦30,000 + transport ₦20,000 = gross ₦120,000.
  • Pension (employee): 8% × ₦120,000 = ₦9,600. (The employer separately pays 10% × ₦120,000 = ₦12,000 into the same retirement savings account.)
  • Annual chargeable income: gross ₦1,440,000 − pension ₦115,200 = ₦1,324,800 (no NHF, no rent relief declared).
  • Tax for the year: first ₦800,000 × 0% = ₦0; remaining ₦524,800 × 15% = ₦78,720.
  • PAYE on the payslip: ₦78,720 ÷ 12 = ₦6,560 a month.
  • Net pay: ₦120,000 − ₦9,600 − ₦6,560 = ₦103,840. Total deductions of ₦16,160 are 13% of wages — well inside the one-third cap.
  • If the same employee declared rent of ₦400,000 a year: relief of ₦80,000 would bring chargeable income to ₦1,244,800 and the year’s tax to ₦66,720 — ₦5,560 a month. The declaration is the employee’s to make, with the rent receipt as proof (see the rent-relief guide).

If you are the employee reading yours

  • Add the earnings and subtract the deductions yourself. The net on the slip must equal the transfer in your bank; a difference is a question for payroll, in writing.
  • Check the PAYE against the bands. On ₦120,000 a month the right figure is about ₦6,560; a payslip still taking the old 7%-and-up rates on the first naira is taking too much.
  • Check the pension reached the account. 8% from you and 10% from the employer should appear in your retirement savings account statement each month; the deduction on the slip is not the same as the remittance.
  • NHF: did you opt in? If you are in the private sector and never chose to contribute, the 2.5% line should not be there.
  • Fines and “shortages” are not deductions. A Labour Act worker may not be fined through the payslip (s.5(1)); raise it in writing, and keep the slip.
  • Keep twelve months of slips. Lenders, landlords and embassies ask for three to six; the tax authority may ask for the year.

Questions people ask

What should a payslip in Nigeria contain?

Employer and employee details, the pay period and pay date, each earning line and the gross, each deduction by name — PAYE, pension, NHF where the employee contributes, anything else the employee agreed to in writing — the total deductions, and the net pay in figures and words. The sample above is that layout; the generator produces it.

How is PAYE calculated on a salary in 2026?

Annual gross less pension (and NHF, NHIS and rent relief where they apply) gives chargeable income; the first ₦800,000 is taxed at 0%, the next ₦2.2 million at 15%, the next ₦9 million at 18%, then 21%, 23% and 25% above ₦50 million (Nigeria Tax Act 2025, Fourth Schedule). The year’s tax divided by twelve is the monthly PAYE. A worker earning the national minimum wage or less pays none (s.163).

Is NHF compulsory for private-sector employees?

No — not since February 2023. Section 45 of the Business Facilitation (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 2023 amended s.4 of the NHF Act: public-sector employees and the self-employed earning the minimum wage or more “shall” contribute 2.5% of monthly income; private-sector employees “may”. An employer deducts it only for an employee who has opted in.

What is the pension deduction on a payslip?

At least 8% of monthly emoluments from the employee, and at least 10% from the employer on top, both paid into the employee’s retirement savings account (Pension Reform Act 2014, s.4(1)). Emoluments are as the contract defines them, but never less than basic + housing + transport.

Can an employer deduct fines or shortages on the payslip?

Not from a Labour Act worker. Section 5(1) forbids deductions in respect of fines; a deduction for loss or damage caused by wilful misconduct needs an authorised labour officer’s prior written consent; and all deductions in a month may not exceed one-third of wages (s.5(7)).

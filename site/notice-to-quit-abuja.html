What the law actually says in Abuja (FCT)

The statute: Recovery of Premises Act — Cap. 544, Laws of the Federation of Nigeria (Abuja) 1990 — also cited as LFN 2004. Commenced 1990.

Although an Act of the National Assembly, it is not a federal law of general application. It was made specially for the Federal Capital Territory. There is no federal landlord-and-tenant statute for Nigeria as a whole.

Where a dispute goes

Recovery follows the Act through the FCT courts — Magistrates’/District Courts and the FCT High Court. One practitioner source puts the line at an annual rental value of ₦5,000,000 — District Court at or below, FCT High Court above. This figure rests on a single source and is shown on the page as such (tier C).

How notice works here

  • No half-yearly tier. Unlike Lagos s.13(1)(d), the Act does not prescribe a separate period for a half-yearly tenant. A tenancy exceeding one year is treated as a yearly tenancy, for which six months’ notice is sufficient.
  • No arrears shortcut. Lagos s.13(2)–(3) and the Cross River and Kwara provisos let a tenancy lapse on defined arrears with no quit notice. The FCT Act has no equivalent — serve the notice.
  • The date of service is what counts, not the date written on the notice. The full statutory period must fall between service and the stated expiry; a month means one calendar month and a year means six calendar months, and no less.
  • s.7 — once the term has ended or been determined by a written notice to quit in Form B, C or D, the landlord serves written notice in Form E of intention to proceed to recover possession, dated not less than seven days from the date of service. That notice cannot be served before the quit notice expires.
  • s.31 attaches the Forms. s.2 defines “agent” as a person usually employed by the landlord in letting or collecting rent, or specially authorised in writing under the landlord’s hand — so an agent or solicitor needs written authority before issuing a valid notice.

Service: Where the defendant cannot be found, his dwelling is unknown, or admission cannot be obtained for service, posting a copy of the process on some conspicuous part of the premises is deemed good service.

Other protections worth knowing

  • A landlord may not resort to self-help. Once the seven days expire the occupier becomes a statutory tenant, removable only by a lawful court order.
  • s.20 — the Court may issue a warrant for possession even where a counterclaim is undetermined or unsatisfied.
  • A landlord who obtains an order for possession by intentionally providing false information commits an offence, and the Court may order reasonable compensation to the tenant.

When a notice to quit is valid in Abuja

  • Correct minimum period — see the table above; a yearly tenant needs six months where the agreement is silent.
  • Proper service — it must reach the tenant in a way you can prove. Service can be on any day and at any hour, but proof is everything.
  • Signed by the right person — the landlord, or an agent/solicitor with written authority dated before the notice is issued.
  • After it expires: a further 7 days’ notice of the owner’s intention to apply to court to recover possession — then the case goes to the District Courts and the High Court of the FCT, Abuja.
  • Fixed term already expired? No quit notice is needed at all — only the 7-day court notice.

Questions people ask

Can my landlord give me less than six months’ notice?

If your written agreement specifies a shorter (or longer) notice, that clause generally governs. Where it is silent, the statutory minimums apply.

What if I’m in arrears?

No arrears shortcut. Lagos s.13(2)–(3) and the Cross River and Kwara provisos let a tenancy lapse on defined arrears with no quit notice. The FCT Act has no equivalent — serve the notice.

How do I count the notice period?

Traditionally it had to expire at the end of a complete period of the tenancy — the modern Lagos rule allows expiry on or after the current term ends. When in doubt, give more than the minimum.

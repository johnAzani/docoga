Is the caution fee refundable in Nigeria? Yes — here is how to actually get it back

The caution fee is the most disputed money in Nigerian renting. It is usually 10% of annual rent or one to three months' rent, paid on top of everything else — and too many tenants never see it again. The short answer is that it is refundable money. Whether you get it back depends almost entirely on what you wrote down before you moved in.

The short answer

Yes — a caution fee is a refundable security deposit, not a payment for anything. It is held against damage you cause beyond normal wear and tear, or bills you leave unpaid. At the end of the tenancy you are entitled to it back, minus the documented cost of genuine damage. If an agreement describes the caution fee as non-refundable, push back before you sign — that is a red flag, not a standard term.

What the law actually says

  • Nigeria has no single national tenancy statute — tenancy is regulated state by state, so there is no one refund rule that applies everywhere.
  • In Lagos the 2011 Law has no explicit refund framework. It requires the landlord to issue a separate receipt for a security deposit (never lump it into the rent receipt) and entitles the tenant to a written account at least every six months of how money paid for deposits, services and service charges was spent. It does not itself spell out the refund.
  • That gap is exactly why the written clause matters. Because the statute is quiet, your agreement and your receipt are the evidence a court will look at.
  • A 2026 Lagos Tenancy Bill would change this — the draft entitles the tenant to a refund of the deposit at the end of the tenancy, minus documented repair costs. It is a bill, not law yet. Until it passes, the 2011 Law and your agreement are what count.

Protect the deposit at move-in, not at move-out

  • Receipt the deposit separately from rent — a receipt that names it a caution fee, with the amount in figures and words.
  • Get the refund terms in writing in the tenancy agreement. A clause that works: “The Tenant shall pay a refundable caution deposit of ₦[amount]. On lawful termination of the tenancy and delivery of vacant possession, the Landlord shall refund the deposit within [14/21] days, less only the reasonable, documented cost of repairing damage caused by the Tenant beyond fair wear and tear. Any deduction shall be itemised in writing with supporting receipts.”
  • Record the property's condition on day one — dated photos and a short checklist of every existing fault. This settles most arguments before they start.
  • Insist on itemised deductions. A landlord who deducts “for repairs” without a list and receipts has not made a lawful deduction.

If the landlord simply refuses

  • Send a written demand letter with the receipt, the agreement clause, your move-in photos and the itemised breakdown you are disputing. Most refusals end here.
  • Keep every payment record. Where the landlord refuses to accept rent at all, Lagos tenants can apply to pay it into the court's registry (s.28 of the Tenancy Law) — so “the landlord wouldn't take my money” is not a dead end.
  • Then the court. A money claim of this size is well within the small-claims procedure in Lagos; confirm the current monetary limit with the court or a lawyer.

Questions people ask

Is the caution fee the same as rent?

No. It is a refundable deposit held against damage or unpaid charges — it is not the landlord's income, and it should never appear inside the rent figure on your receipt.

How much can a landlord charge as a caution fee?

It is not fixed by statute in most states. Market practice in Lagos is roughly 10% of annual rent, or one to three months' rent. Whatever you agree must be written into the tenancy agreement and receipted separately.

Can a landlord deduct for 'wear and tear'?

No. Fair wear and tear — faded paint, worn fittings from normal use — is the landlord's cost. Deductions are only for damage you actually caused, and they must be itemised with receipts.

What if I never got a receipt for the deposit?

You can still claim — bank transfers, WhatsApp messages and the tenancy agreement all count as evidence. But this is precisely why the separate receipt matters; ask for one now, in writing, rather than at move-out.

How long does the landlord have to refund it?

The statute does not set a deadline, which is why your agreement should. Fourteen to twenty-one days after delivery of vacant possession is the commonly agreed window.

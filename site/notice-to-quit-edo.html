What the law actually says in Edo

The statute: Rent Control and Recovery of Residential Premises Edict 1977 — Edict No. 4 of 1977, Bendel State of Nigeria — Cap. 74, Laws of Edo State. Commenced 1 March 1977.

The enactment still reads “Bendel State”: Bendel was split into Edo and Delta in 1991, and the Edict is received as Edo law. An academic paper cites it as “Cap R1”; the Judiciary’s own PDF carries “Cap. 74”. We use Cap. 74 and note the alternative.

Where a dispute goes

Edo is a tribunal state, not a magistrates’-court state. s.4 establishes Rent Tribunals constituted of (a) a chairman who has been in practice as a legal practitioner in Nigeria for not less than five years, and (b) two other persons of probity, appointed by the Governor. The chairman plus one other member is a quorum (s.4(4)), and the tribunal may sit with assessors where it thinks it necessary (s.4(6)).

s.41(2) repealed the Recovery of Premises Law to the extent that it relates to accommodation in residential premises, and s.41(3) repealed the Magistrates’ Court Law provisions on landlord-and-tenant actions over residential accommodation. s.2 defines “court” to include the High Court, magistrates’ courts and customary courts of the State.

Not yet verified. The Edict’s graduated notice periods sit in s.18, and the subsection text has not yet been read. We therefore do not publish a figure for Edo. What is verified is the rule of validity: a notice is ineffective where the time between the giving of the notice and the determination of the tenancy is less than the period prescribed in s.18.

Grounds a landlord can rely on

  • the rent lawfully due is in arrear for one month after it became due — Edo — one month
  • the tenant has breached a covenant or agreement of the tenancy
  • the tenant gave notice to quit and, in consequence, the landlord contracted to sell or let the premises or otherwise took steps whereby he would be seriously prejudiced if he could not obtain possession

The tribunal may order possession without proof of suitable alternative accommodation where it considers it reasonable to do so. Note the contrast: Edo’s arrears trigger is one month, Cross River’s is three, and Kwara requires suitable alternative accommodation (or a Second Schedule ground) before making any possession order at all.

Prescribed forms

  • Form A — general title of proceedings (In the Rent Tribunals, … District)
  • Form B — notice to quit signed by the landlord himself
  • Form C — notice to quit given by an agent or legal practitioner
  • Form D — notice by landlord to quit lodgings
  • Form E — notice to tenant of owner’s intention to apply to recover possession (seven clear days from service)
  • Form F — writ or plaint
  • Form G — summons for recovery of possession
  • Forms H and I — appointment and report of appraisers (for valuing improvements)
  • Forms J, K, L, M — judgments
  • Warrant for possession

Under Form G, a sub-tenant served with a summons who fails to notify his immediate landlord is liable to forfeit three years’ rent of the premises he holds.

Protection from retaliatory notices

Edo’s strongest tenant protection, and one of the strongest in the country. s.16(1): once a tenant has applied to the tribunal, any quit notice served afterwards is of no effect, and no similar notice may be given before the tribunal’s decision. s.16(2): the tribunal may declare a quit notice invalid and of no effect if it was not issued in good faith, and its endorsement to that effect on the notice is sufficient evidence of the decision before any court.

A tenant who takes a genuine dispute to the tribunal cannot be punished for it with a retaliatory quit notice.

A new tenant’s first six months

Where rent is payable in advance for a monthly or quarterly tenancy, the landlord may not give notice to quit or recover possession during the first six months of the tenancy — except on the order of the tribunal or with the tenant’s consent. A new Edo tenant who has paid in advance has a six-month statutory breathing space.

When a notice to quit is valid in Edo State (Benin City)

  • Correct minimum period — see the table above; a yearly tenant needs six months where the agreement is silent.
  • Proper service — it must reach the tenant in a way you can prove. Service can be on any day and at any hour, but proof is everything.
  • Signed by the right person — the landlord, or an agent/solicitor with written authority dated before the notice is issued.
  • After it expires: a further 7 days’ notice of the owner’s intention to apply to court to recover possession — then the case goes to the Rent Tribunals of Edo State.
  • Fixed term already expired? No quit notice is needed at all — only the 7-day court notice.

Questions people ask

Can my landlord give me less than six months’ notice?

If your written agreement specifies a shorter (or longer) notice, that clause generally governs. Where it is silent, the statutory minimums apply.

What if I’m in arrears?

Serious arrears can cost a tenant the protection of a quit notice — courts can order possession on proof of the arrears. Never let arrears run; and landlords: follow the lawful process, never self-help.

How do I count the notice period?

Traditionally it had to expire at the end of a complete period of the tenancy — the modern Lagos rule allows expiry on or after the current term ends. When in doubt, give more than the minimum.

Nigeria’s rent index — built from rent actually paid, not asking prices

Nobody in Nigeria measures the rent people actually pay. The Bureau of Statistics publishes a national price index, not rents by state; the private “average rent” figures come from listings — what landlords ask, not what tenants pay. Why Nigeria has no rent index yet

Three things have to be true for a rent index to exist: the rent has to be written down, the paper has to be dated and placed, and somebody has to collect it at scale without collecting the tenant. In Nigeria the first step fails — most rent changes hands against a transfer alert or a handshake, so there is nothing to count.

  • What exists: the National Bureau of Statistics’ Consumer Price Index, which has a housing component but is a national price index, not a rent series, and not by state.
  • What the “average rent in Lagos” articles use: asking prices scraped from property listings. Asking is not paid — listings over-represent the top of the market and vanish once a flat is let.
  • What a real index needs: the amount actually paid, the period it covers, the state, the date — which is exactly what a lawful rent receipt carries (Lagos Tenancy Law 2011, s.5(2)).

How the DocOga Rent Monitor works

  • One receipt, one observation. When a landlord or agent prints a DocOga rent receipt with the free verification code, the ledger stores five fields: state, kind of payment, amount, period in months, date. Nothing else.
  • No person in the data. No name, address, phone number, IP address or cookie is recorded — only a one-way fingerprint of the receipt’s words, computed on the issuer’s own phone, so the code can be checked later. Data that cannot be related to an individual is outside the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (s.65), and the ledger is built to the Act’s minimisation rule (s.24(1)(c)) regardless.
  • A cell is a state × kind × calendar month. A cell publishes a rent level only at 30 receipts or more; below that, counts only. Above it: the median and the inter-quartile range, never an average — rents are heavy-tailed and one penthouse would move a mean.
  • Annualised before comparison: amount ÷ months × 12, so a six-month payment and a two-year payment sit on the same scale. Receipts without a period are counted but excluded from the level.
  • Published in the open. The counts on this page come live from the same public endpoint the verification page uses; the method is written here before the first number, so nobody can accuse the first number of being chosen.

Questions people ask

Is my rent amount visible to anyone?

No. Amounts are never shown individually — only as a median across 30 or more receipts in a state, kind and month. The fingerprint that lets a code be checked is never published and cannot be reversed into names or addresses, which the ledger never had.

Does registering a receipt cost anything?

No. The verification code, the QR and the check page are free on the free tier. The Oga Pass only removes the banner from the printed document.

What is the difference between asking rent and paid rent?

Asking rent is the figure in a listing; paid rent is what the tenant actually handed over after negotiation, inclusive of any agreed discount or increase. Listing-based “average rent” figures measure the first; a receipt measures the second.

When will the first rent level be published?

When any state reaches 30 verified receipts of one kind in one calendar month. The live table above shows each state’s closest cell and how far it is from 30.

Can a landlord be identified from the ledger?

No. The ledger holds state, kind, amount, period, date and a one-way fingerprint. Even DocOga cannot tell whose receipt a code belongs to — only that a receipt with exactly those words was printed.

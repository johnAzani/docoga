Query letter in Nigeria — the sample query, the employee’s reply, the warning letter that follows, and the law under all three

Updated 12 November 2026 · DocOga

An employee resumes late twice, or the till is short, or a customer complains. The first letter is not a warning — it is a query: a request to explain in writing, by a stated time, before any decision is taken. This page carries all three letters of a lawful disciplinary paper trail in the standard Nigerian form — the query, the employee’s reply, and the first written warning — with square brackets where the details go. [Business address, Lagos]

Dear [Employee's first name],

It has been reported that [you resumed work at 9:40am on Monday 10 November

12 November 2026]), which, if established, is contrary to the terms of your

You are required to explain in writing why disciplinary action should not be
taken against you in respect of the above, and to submit your explanation to

Your explanation will be considered before any decision is taken. Failure to
respond within the time given may result in a decision being taken on the

applicable labour laws.

What the warning may not do (Labour Act, Cap L1 LFN 2004): impose a fine or a
deduction from wages for the misconduct — s.5(1) forbids both unless a law
permits them or an authorised labour officer consents in writing to a
reasonable deduction for loss the worker's wilful misconduct caused — and in
any month deductions of every kind may not exceed one-third of wages (s.5(7)).
Summary dismissal without notice remains open only for conduct that justifies
it (s.11(5)); a documented query, reply and warning are what show, later, that
it did.

What each letter is for

  • The query states the allegation and its dates and asks for a written explanation by a stated time. It must not announce the punishment — a query that says “you are hereby warned” is a warning with the hearing skipped. Hand it over in person, have the employee sign for receipt, and keep the copy.
  • The reply is the employee’s hearing. It should say what happened, why, and what will change; it should not argue with the manager or accuse colleagues. Late, missing or evasive replies are themselves facts the employer may rely on.
  • The warning records the conduct, refers to the query and the reply, states the expected improvement and a deadline, and says what follows if it is not met. “First”, “second” and “final” are not in the Act; they are the ladder most Nigerian staff handbooks use, and the generator writes all three.
  • The acknowledgement at the foot of the query and the warning is the proof of service. The words “receipt does not indicate agreement” are there so the employee has no reason to refuse to sign.

The five steps and the law under each

StepWhat it isWhat the Labour Act says
1. QueryA letter stating what is alleged, with dates, asking the employee to explain in writing by a stated time (24–72 hours is usual). It decides nothing.Nothing in the Act requires a query; what the Act fixes is what the employer may not do instead — fine the worker or dock wages for the misconduct (s.5(1)).
2. ReplyThe employee’s written explanation: what happened, why, what changes. Short, factual, dated, a copy kept.The Act is silent on the reply; a reply that is considered before the next step is what makes that step defensible if it is ever challenged.
3. WarningFirst, second or final written warning: the conduct, the expected improvement, a deadline, and what follows if it is not met. Signed for receipt.A warning may not carry a fine or a deduction (s.5(1)); whatever deductions the law does allow may not exceed one-third of a month’s wages (s.5(7)). Special conditions of the job belong in the written terms (s.7(1)(h)).
4. Termination on noticeWhere warnings have not worked: notice as the contract provides, or payment in lieu.Notice of at least one day, one week, two weeks or one month by length of service (s.11(2)); payment in lieu allowed (s.11(6)); all wages due must be paid on or before the notice expires (s.11(7)).
5. Summary dismissalDismissal without notice for gross misconduct — theft, fraud, violence, wilful damage.Kept open by s.11(5) for conduct that would have justified it at common law. The query and the reply are what show, later, that the conduct was established and the worker was heard.

The Act’s floors bind a “worker” as s.91 defines one — administrative, executive, technical and professional staff are governed by their contracts. For them, the steps above are still the practice the National Industrial Court expects to see on paper.

What an employer may not do instead of a warning

  • Fine the worker. “No employer shall make any deduction … or any agreement or contract with a worker for any deduction … for or in respect of any fines” (s.5(1)). A “lateness fine” in a staff handbook is unenforceable against a Labour Act worker.
  • Dock wages for the loss. A deduction for loss caused by the worker’s wilful misconduct or neglect needs the prior written consent of an authorised labour officer and must be reasonable (s.5(1), proviso). Short till, broken stock, a lost phone — none may simply be taken from salary.
  • Exceed a third. Whatever deductions the law does permit — PAYE, pension, union dues, an overpayment within the last three months (s.5(5)) — may not together exceed one-third of the month’s wages (s.5(7)).
  • Suspend without pay indefinitely. The Act does not provide for it; a suspension pending the reply should be short, in writing, and on pay unless the contract clearly says otherwise.
  • Dismiss summarily for an ordinary lapse. Lateness, a missed target or a rude word are not the gross misconduct s.11(5) contemplates; they are query-and-warning matters. Summary dismissal is for theft, fraud, violence and the like — and even then the query first is what proves it later.

If you are the employee who received a query

  • Reply in writing, inside the time given. Silence is read against you. If the time is too short to gather facts, say so in writing and ask for a day.
  • Facts, then responsibility, then the fix. The sample reply above is the shape: what happened, what you did about it at the time, what you accept, what will change. One page.
  • Sign for receipt. Signing the acknowledgement is not admitting the allegation — the letter says so. Refusing to sign only removes your proof of the date you received it.
  • Keep everything. The query, your reply, the warning. If the matter ends in a dismissal you dispute, the National Industrial Court will ask whether you were heard; these are the papers that answer.
  • Check your payslip afterwards. A fine or an unexplained deduction following a warning is itself unlawful for a Labour Act worker (s.5(1)) — raise it in writing.

Questions people ask

What is a query letter?

In Nigerian workplaces, a query is the employer’s letter asking an employee to explain in writing, by a stated time, why disciplinary action should not be taken over a stated allegation. It is not a warning and it decides nothing; the reply is considered before the next step. The sample above is that letter; the generator writes it with your facts.

How do I reply to a query letter?

In writing, before the deadline, in one page: acknowledge the query and its date, state what happened and why, say what you did at the time, accept what is yours to accept, state what will change, and sign and date it. Keep a copy. The sample reply above is that letter.

Can my employer fine me or deduct from my salary after a query?

Not for a Labour Act worker. Section 5(1) forbids deductions or agreements for deductions “in respect of any fines”; a deduction for loss caused by wilful misconduct needs an authorised labour officer’s prior written consent and must be reasonable; and all deductions in a month are capped at one-third of wages (s.5(7)).

How many warnings before termination in Nigeria?

The Labour Act sets no number. It fixes the notice a termination needs — one day to one month by length of service (s.11(2)) or payment in lieu (s.11(6)) — and keeps summary dismissal for conduct that justifies it (s.11(5)). The first-second-final ladder comes from staff handbooks and from what the National Industrial Court expects to see when a dismissal is challenged.

Must I sign the acknowledgement on a query or warning?

Signing records receipt, not agreement — the letter says so. Refusing only deprives you of proof of the date you received it, which is the date your reply time runs from.

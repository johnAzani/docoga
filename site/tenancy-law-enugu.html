Tenancy Law in Enugu State: Notice Periods, Rent, Courts

Updated 3 November 2026 · law shelf reviewed 3 November 2026 · DocOga

Which law governs a tenancy in Enugu State, how many months’ notice it requires, what it says about receipts, advance rent and caution money, and which court or tribunal hears a dispute. Notice-to-quit minimums in Enugu State

Verified against Landlord and Tenant Law — Cap. 101, Revised Laws of Enugu State 2004. Where the agreement is silent, the minimum notice is:

We have not verified an Enugu advance-rent cap of the Lagos s.4 kind. Read so far (3 Nov 2026): the 2008 amendment as quoted regulates reviews, not advance rent; the 2025 bill text regulates agents’ and legal fees, not advance rent; Cap. 101 itself has not been read. Record whatever advance is agreed in the tenancy agreement before money moves.

Rent running out does not end the tenancy. A tenancy ends by a valid notice or by the expiry of a fixed term, not by the paid period ending. A landlord who treats the end of the paid rent as the end of the tenancy and moves to eject is acting unlawfully — notice first, then the seven-day notice, then court.

What the law actually says in Enugu

The statute: Landlord and Tenant Law — Cap. 101, Revised Laws of Enugu State 2004. Commenced 27 August 1991.

The principal law. The Court of Appeal in Amah v. Ozouli [2010] NGCA 6 holds that the law applicable in Enugu is the Landlord and Tenant Law, Cap. 101, Revised Laws of Enugu State, which came into operation on 27 August 1991 — 1991 is the commencement, 2004 the revised edition. It is the Enugu sibling of Anambra’s Cap. 76, from the state Enugu was carved out of in 1991.

Where a dispute goes

Recovery of possession in Enugu runs through the courts under the Landlord and Tenant Law, Cap. 101. We have not read the section that names the forum, so we do not say whether it is the Magistrates’ Court, a Rent Tribunal or both. The Assembly’s 2008 announcement of the amendment said the law lets landlords and tenants “seek redress at the Rent Tribunal” — a single source, recorded here and not asserted. The High Court retains jurisdiction in every state (s.272 of the 1999 Constitution).

A correction to our own research

A correction to our own earlier pages.

Until 3 November 2026 our pages said there was no percentage cap on rent increases anywhere in Nigeria. Enugu has one. The Landlord and Tenant (Amendment) Law 2008 caps any rent review at 15% of the existing rent and allows a review only after every second anniversary of the tenancy. The national sentence has been corrected on every page that carried it; Lagos, the FCT, Rivers, Anambra and Kano still have no such cap.

Rent increases

In Enugu a rent increase may not exceed 15% of the existing rent, and rent may be reviewed only after every second anniversary of the tenancy — s.3(b) of the Landlord and Tenant (Amendment) Law 2008, which replaced s.109 of Cap. 101. Enugu is the one Nigerian state where we have verified a statutory cap on rent increases.

The section as quoted: “rent chargeable for letting of any accommodation within the State shall be as agreed between the landlord and the tenant, and may be reviewed by the parties after every second anniversary of the tenancy created … PROVIDED that the difference between the new and the existing rent shall not exceed 15% of the existing rent”.

Three conditions, all from the section: (1) the review is by the parties — a unilateral increase is ineffective (Jovinco Nigeria Ltd v. Ibeozimako (2014) LPELR-23599(CA): rent increment must be supported by an agreement; Cobra Ltd v. Omole Estate (2000) 1 NWLR (Pt. 655) 1); (2) not before the second anniversary; (3) not more than 15% above the existing rent.

Worked example: a flat let at ₦400,000 a year from 1 January 2025 cannot be reviewed before 1 January 2027, and the new rent may not exceed ₦460,000 (₦400,000 plus 15%). A demand to move from ₦400,000 to ₦600,000 at a review — a 50% rise of the kind reported from Amechi in 2025 — breaches the 15% ceiling even where the second anniversary has passed.

Lagos has no cap: its remedy is the s.37 application to have an increase declared unreasonable. The FCT, Rivers, Anambra and Kano have neither a cap nor a verified s.37 equivalent. Enugu’s cap applies to a sitting tenancy being reviewed; it says nothing about the rent a landlord may ask of a new tenant, which remains “as agreed”.

How we know it, and what we have not seen. The section is quoted in full by a Lagos practitioner (2021) and the Assembly announced the same two-year/15% rule, replacing a 25% ceiling, when it passed the amendment on 13 August 2008. We have not read the enrolled 2008 Law or a reported judgment applying the 15% figure. Two independent sources agree; it is published as tier B.

A bill that would change this — not law yet

Enugu State Landlord and Tenant (Amendment) Bill (2025 private member bill; executive bill announced 26 October 2026)

A bill, not law, as at 3 November 2026. Two bills would amend the Landlord and Tenant Law, Cap. 101: the 2025 private member bill (first reading 25 February 2025, public hearing 26 March 2025, then with the Attorney-General) and the executive bill the Speaker announced on 26 October 2026, which Governor Peter Mbah pledged to see passed within four weeks. Neither had passed on 3 November 2026.

Where it stands: executive bill announced 26 October 2026 with a four-week pledge — so the window to watch is late November 2026. Until the Governor assents and the law is gazetted, the 2008 amendment (rent review only after every second anniversary, no more than 15%) is the newest Enugu law.

What that means today: a landlord who says “Enugu has banned caution fees” is ahead of the law; one who demands a fee is not yet breaking it. DocOga’s law watch reads the news for this bill every week and flags a passage report the day it appears.

A related jurisdiction

Anambra’s Cap. 76 is the sibling statute. Enugu was carved out of Anambra in 1991 and its Cap. 101 descends from the same Landlord and Tenant Law; the Court of Appeal in Amah v. Ozouli dealt with both. Anambra’s s.173 alternative-accommodation rule is Anambra’s and is not attributed to Enugu here until Cap. 101 is read.

Other protections worth knowing

  • A landlord may not resort to self-help — no lock-outs, no removal of goods or roofs, no disconnection. Unlawful eviction sounds in damages (Ihenacho v. Uzochukwu (1997) 1 SCNJ 117; Chicason Group v. Otero [2021] 6 NWLR (Pt. 1772) 222).
  • A rent increase that ignores the 2008 section — before the second anniversary, above 15%, or imposed without agreement — is an unlawful increment, and a court will not enforce the increased figure.
  • A defect in the issue, content or service of a notice vitiates the whole proceeding; the date of service is the material date, not the date written on the notice.

Questions people ask

Is there a tenancy law in Enugu State?

The block below names the statute we verified for Enugu State with its citation and sections — or, where we could not cite one from a source we trust, says so and sets out the general Nigerian position that applies instead.

How many months’ notice does a landlord have to give in Enugu State?

Where the agreement is silent: one week for a tenant at will, one month for a monthly tenant, three months for a quarterly tenant and six months for a yearly tenant — the table above shows the Enugu State ladder with its section. A written agreement can fix its own period.

Can my landlord throw me out without going to court in Enugu State?

No. Across Nigeria possession is recovered through the courts of Enugu State (the Law’s own forum section has not been read) after the notice to quit and the 7 days’ notice have expired. Lock-outs, removing doors or roofs and seizing belongings are self-help the courts do not allow.

Does the agreement override the law in Enugu State?

On notice periods, generally yes — the statutory periods apply where the agreement is silent. Protections written as offences or prohibitions (for example a receipt duty or an advance-rent cap) cannot be contracted out of.

Tenancy Agreement in Nigeria: the 2026 format, clause by clause

A Nigerian tenancy agreement is the document that decides who pays what, for how long, and how either side ends the arrangement. Most disputes between Nigerian landlords and tenants are really arguments about a clause that was never written down. This page sets out the standard format and the rules that decide whether your agreement holds up — and DocOga will build the whole document for you in about two minutes, free, on your phone.

The clauses a Nigerian tenancy agreement must contain

  • The parties — full legal names and addresses of the landlord and the tenant (and the agent, if one is collecting money).
  • The premises — description and location, including the specific unit or flat.
  • The term — start date and end date, or the period (monthly, quarterly, yearly).
  • The rent — amount in figures and words, when it is payable, and how it is paid.
  • The advance — how much is payable up front, within the legal limit (below).
  • The caution fee — amount, that it is a refundable deposit, and the refund terms.
  • The notice period — the notice each side must give to end the tenancy. This is the single most valuable clause in the document.
  • Covenants — repairs, utilities and service charge, subletting, permitted use, alterations, and what happens at renewal.
  • Signatures and witnesses — both parties, dated, each with a witness.

The rules that decide whether it holds up

  • The advance-rent cap. Section 4 of the Lagos Tenancy Law 2011 makes it unlawful for a landlord or agent to demand or receive more than one year's rent in advance from a yearly tenant, or six months from a monthly tenant. Anything above that is unlawful — the tenant is at fault too if they pay it.
  • Notice is what you write. The statutory minimums apply only where there is no stipulation. If you want three months' notice on a yearly tenancy, the agreement must say three months.
  • An oral tenancy still exists. Under the Lagos law a tenancy is deemed to exist where premises are granted for value — express or implied, oral or written. But an oral tenancy leaves you with no agreed rent record and no caution-fee terms.
  • State law, not one national law. Nigeria has no single national tenancy statute. Lagos runs the Tenancy Law 2011; the FCT runs the Recovery of Premises Act; other states run their own recovery-of-premises legislation.
  • Check the state page for your location. Where the Lagos law applies: the Lagos Tenancy Law 2011 covers most of Lagos State, but s.1(3) excludes four areas: Apapa, Ikeja GRA, Ikoyi and Victoria Island (plus residential premises of educational institutions, emergency shelters, care/hospice, hospital and mental-health facilities, and rehabilitative or therapeutic treatment settings).

Questions people ask

Is a written tenancy agreement compulsory in Nigeria?

No — a tenancy can exist without one. But without it you have no agreed rent record, no caution-fee terms, and the statutory notice defaults govern instead of the notice you actually wanted. A two-minute written agreement is cheaper than any dispute.

How much can a landlord ask for in advance?

In Lagos, no more than one year's rent from a yearly tenant or six months from a monthly tenant — s.4 of the Tenancy Law 2011. Other states do not all have the same cap, so record whatever advance you agree clearly in the agreement before money moves.

Does the agreement override the notice periods in the law?

Generally yes. The statutory minimums apply where the agreement is silent. Write the notice you want into the agreement and both sides are bound by it.

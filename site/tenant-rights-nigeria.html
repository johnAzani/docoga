Rights of a tenant in Nigeria — and the landlord obligations that back them

Most Nigerian tenants do not know what they are legally owed — which is why disputes get settled by whoever shouts loudest. The rights below are enforceable, and they come from the Lagos Tenancy Law 2011 (the most detailed state law) and the recovery-of-premises legislation other states and the FCT apply. Knowing them changes how a conversation with your landlord goes.

Six rights every Nigerian tenant has

  • 1. Lawful notice before you can be asked to leave. Where the agreement is silent: 1 week (tenant at will or weekly), 1 month (monthly), 3 months (quarterly or half-yearly), 6 months (yearly) — then a further 7 days' notice of the owner's intention to recover possession before court.
  • 2. No forceful ejection. Only a court can order possession. In Lagos, s.44 makes forcible ejection, threats or molestation aimed at ejecting a tenant, removing a roof, changing locks, or wilful damage a criminal offence — fine up to ₦250,000 or up to six months' imprisonment.
  • 3. A receipt for every payment. Rent, deposit and service charge each get their own receipt; refusing a rent receipt carries a ₦10,000 fine in Lagos.
  • 4. Quiet enjoyment. The landlord must not terminate or restrict a common facility or service for the premises, and must not seize your property or interfere with your access to it (s.7).
  • 5. Repairs of the structure and common parts. The landlord must effect repairs and maintain the external and common parts of the premises (s.7).
  • 6. The advance-rent cap. A Lagos landlord or agent may not demand or receive more than one year's rent in advance from a yearly tenant, or six months from a monthly tenant (s.4).

Landlord obligations in the same breath

  • Deliver the premises in a tenantable condition and keep the external and common parts repaired.
  • Not to cut off facilities or services, and not to seize the tenant's belongings.
  • To issue receipts — rent, and separately for any deposit, service or facility charge.
  • To give a written account at least every six months of how deposit and service-charge money was spent.
  • To follow the lawful process to recover possession: notice to quit, then 7 days' notice, then court.
  • For business premises (Lagos, s.9): not to inhibit the tenant's access in any substantial manner, and not to substantially alter or inhibit the flow of customers to the premises.

And the tenant's side

  • Pay the rent at the time and in the manner agreed — arrears are the fastest route to losing the protection of a quit notice.
  • Keep the premises in good and tenantable repair, fair wear and tear excepted.
  • Pay the rates and charges that are the tenant's responsibility under the agreement.
  • Not to sublet, assign or alter the premises without the landlord's written consent.
  • Not to use the premises for an illegal or immoral purpose.
  • To give up possession lawfully at the end of the term.

If your landlord breaks the rules

  • Put everything in writing — WhatsApp messages are admissible evidence and they are dated.
  • Self-help against a self-help landlord is still self-help. Do not withhold rent unilaterally; in Lagos you can apply to pay it into the court's registry if the landlord refuses to accept it (s.28).
  • Forceful ejection is reportable. It is a criminal offence in Lagos, not a civil disagreement.
  • Document everything — receipts, photos, the agreement, the notices you received.

Questions people ask

Can my landlord remove my roof or change the locks to make me leave?

No. In Lagos that is an offence under s.44 — a fine of up to ₦250,000 or up to six months' imprisonment. Possession can only be recovered by a court order.

Do I still pay rent during the notice period?

Yes. A six-month notice is not six free months — rent continues to be due throughout, and after expiry the landlord can claim mesne profits for the period you remain.

I have no written agreement. Do I have any rights?

Yes. Under the Lagos law a tenancy is deemed to exist where premises are granted for value, whether oral or written. The statutory notice minimums and protections still apply — you just have no agreed rent record, which is why you should write one now.

Can my landlord raise my rent at any time?

Not mid-term if your agreement fixes the rent for the term. Rent increases properly take effect at renewal, on notice — and the agreement's renewal clause governs. Any increase demanded mid-term without a contractual basis is worth challenging.

Which law applies to me?

State law. Lagos runs the Tenancy Law 2011 (which does not apply in Apapa, Ikeja GRA, Ikoyi or Victoria Island); the FCT runs the Recovery of Premises Act; other states run their own recovery-of-premises legislation.

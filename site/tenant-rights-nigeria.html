Tenant rights in Nigeria — what your landlord cannot do, with the section of the law

Most Nigerian tenants do not know what they are legally owed — which is why disputes get settled by whoever shouts loudest. Everything below carries the section it rests on, from the Lagos Tenancy Law 2011 (the most detailed state law) with the FCT and other states noted where they differ. Knowing the section changes how a conversation with your landlord goes.

Nine things your landlord cannot do — with the section

  • 1. Put you out without a court order. Forcibly ejecting you or attempting to, threatening or molesting you to make you leave, or demolishing, altering or modifying the building to eject you without the court's approval is a criminal offence in Lagos — fine of ₦250,000 or six months' imprisonment (s.44(1)). Removing the roof, changing the locks and taking off the doors all fall inside it.
  • 2. Seize your property or block your access to it — the landlord's statutory obligations include not seizing any item of the tenant's property or interfering with access to it (s.8). This is the answer to "he has locked my things inside".
  • 3. Cut off a facility or service the premises came with — light, water, the borehole, the common generator (s.8).
  • 4. Disturb your quiet enjoyment. s.6 gives you quiet and peaceable enjoyment: privacy, freedom from unreasonable disturbance, exclusive possession (subject only to the landlord's restricted right of inspection) and use of the common areas.
  • 5. Refuse you a receipt. Every rent payment must be receipted, and the receipt must show the date, both names and addresses, the premises, the amount and the period (s.5(1)–(2)); failing to issue it is a ₦100,000 fine (s.5(3)). Deposits and service charges get their own receipt and a written account at least every six months (s.10).
  • 6. Demand more than a year's rent upfront. A sitting yearly tenant may not be asked for more than one year, a sitting monthly tenant more than six months, a new tenant more than one year (s.4(1)–(4)). Both the landlord who collects it and the tenant who pays commit the offence — ₦100,000 or three months (s.4(5)).
  • 7. Shorten your notice. Where the agreement is silent: one week (tenant at will), one month (monthly), three months (quarterly and half-yearly), six months (yearly) (s.13(1)(a)–(e)) — then a further 7 days' notice of the owner's intention to recover possession before court. Full table and a free letter: quit notice — how many months.
  • 8. Eject you while you are challenging a rent increase. s.37 lets a sitting tenant ask the court (Form TL11) to declare an increase unreasonable; it is unlawful to eject the tenant while that application is pending (s.37(4)).
  • 9. Refuse your rent and then call you a defaulter. Where a landlord will not accept rent, the tenant may apply to the court to pay it into the Court Registry (s.29).

These are the minimums where the agreement is silent. Where a written agreement stipulates a notice period, that stipulation generally governs — so the notice you actually want must be written into the agreement.

Lagos s.13(1). Abuja (Recovery of Premises Act, Cap. 544, s.8) runs the same ladder without the half-yearly tier: a tenancy over one year is a yearly tenancy and six calendar months suffice. In both, the period counts from the date of service, not the date written on the letter. After the notice expires, a second notice — seven days, owner's intention to recover possession — and only then court. Arrears change this in Lagos: a monthly tenant six months behind, or a quarterly/half-yearly tenant a year behind, loses the right to a quit notice (s.13(2)–(3)).

Rent increase: what the law actually gives you

  • There is no percentage cap on rent increases in Lagos or anywhere in Nigeria. Any page that quotes one is wrong.
  • Mid-term, the agreement governs. If the rent is fixed for the term, it cannot be raised before the term ends; an increase properly takes effect at renewal, on notice.
  • Your remedy is s.37. Apply to the court on Form TL11 for an order that the increase is unreasonable. The court weighs the general level of rents in the locality, the witnesses and any special circumstances, and may fix the rent at a specific figure (Form TL13). You cannot be ejected while it is pending (s.37(4)).

What the law does NOT promise you — the honest list

  • No "24–48 hours' notice before entry" rule. That figure is copied from foreign tenancy law. What Lagos gives you is privacy and exclusive possession under s.6, with the landlord's right of inspection restricted — so write the inspection notice period into your agreement.
  • No deposit statute. The Lagos law does not use the words "caution fee". What it regulates is the receipt (s.10) and the six-monthly written account of how the money was spent; whether and how it is refunded is what your agreement says. Caution fee: what to write in.
  • No single national tenancy law. The FCT's Recovery of Premises Act is not a federal law of general application; every state has its own. And the Lagos law itself does not apply in Apapa, Ikeja GRA, Ikoyi or Victoria Island (s.1(3)).
  • No right to withhold rent because repairs are outstanding. The landlord must effect repairs and maintain the external and common parts (s.8(vi)); you keep the inside in good and tenantable repair, fair wear and tear excepted (s.7(3)). Withholding rent hands the landlord the arrears case — put the repair demand in writing instead.

And the tenant's side (s.7)

  • Pay the rent at the time and in the manner agreed — arrears are the fastest route to losing the protection of a quit notice.
  • Keep the premises in good and tenantable repair, fair wear and tear excepted (s.7(3)).
  • Pay the rates and charges that are the tenant's responsibility under the agreement.
  • Not to sublet, assign or alter the premises without the landlord's written consent (improvements made with written consent can be claimed for on quitting — s.6(2)).
  • Not to use the premises for an illegal or immoral purpose.
  • To give up possession lawfully at the end of the term.

If your landlord breaks the rules

  • Put everything in writing — WhatsApp messages are admissible evidence and they are dated.
  • Self-help against a self-help landlord is still self-help. Do not withhold rent unilaterally; if the landlord refuses to accept it, apply to pay it into the court's registry (s.29).
  • Forceful ejection is reportable. It is a criminal offence in Lagos (s.44), not a civil disagreement — the police station and the Magistrates' Court, not a shouting match.
  • Where to go: in Lagos the Magistrates' Court (the High Court for high-value premises); there is no Rent Tribunal in Lagos any more. The court may send the case to mediation (s.32); the Citizens Mediation Centre is free.
  • Document everything — receipts, photos, the agreement, the notices you received.

Questions people ask

Can my landlord lock me out or remove my roof if I owe rent?

No. Even in arrears, possession is recovered only through the court. In Lagos forcible ejection, threats to make you leave, or altering the building to eject you is an offence under s.44 — ₦250,000 or six months' imprisonment — and seizing your property or blocking your access to it breaches the landlord's obligations under s.8.

Can my landlord enter my flat without notice?

Lagos law gives you privacy and exclusive possession (s.6) with the landlord's right of inspection restricted, but it does not fix a number of hours' notice — the 24–48 hours quoted online is foreign law. Put the inspection notice period in your tenancy agreement.

Can my landlord increase my rent at any time?

Not mid-term if the agreement fixes the rent for the term; increases properly take effect at renewal, on notice. There is no statutory percentage cap, but a sitting tenant can ask the court under s.37 to declare an increase unreasonable, and cannot be ejected while that application is pending.

How many months' notice must my landlord give me?

Where the agreement is silent: one week for a tenant at will, one month for a monthly tenant, three months for a quarterly or half-yearly tenant, six months for a yearly tenant (Lagos s.13(1); Abuja s.8 of the Recovery of Premises Act is the same without the half-yearly tier). Then a further seven days' notice before court.

Do I still pay rent during the notice period?

Yes. A six-month notice is not six free months — rent continues to be due throughout, and after expiry the landlord can claim mesne profits for the period you remain.

I have no written agreement. Do I have any rights?

Yes. Under the Lagos law a tenancy is deemed to exist where premises are granted for value, whether oral or written. The statutory notice minimums and protections still apply — you just have no agreed rent record, which is why you should write one now.

Is my caution fee or deposit refundable?

The Lagos law does not legislate the refund; it requires a separate receipt for a deposit and a written account every six months of how it was spent (s.10). Refund terms are whatever the agreement says — so write them in before you pay.

Which law applies to me?

State law. Lagos runs the Tenancy Law 2011 (which does not apply in Apapa, Ikeja GRA, Ikoyi or Victoria Island); the FCT runs the Recovery of Premises Act, Cap. 544; other states run their own recovery-of-premises legislation — see the state guides below.

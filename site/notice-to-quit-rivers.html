What the law actually says in Rivers

The statute: Recovery of Premises Law — Cap. 109, Laws of Rivers State of Nigeria, 1999. Commenced 1999.

Governsquit notices and the procedure for recovering possession.

Where a dispute goes

Recovery proceedings run through the courts of Rivers State — a notice to quit in practice issues in the name of the Magistrate Court of Rivers State, in the magisterial district where the premises sit (for example, the Port Harcourt Magisterial District). We have not read the primary text establishing a Rivers Rent Tribunal, and we do not assert that one exists.

How notice works here

  • s.6(2) — the nature of a tenancy is determined, absent contrary evidence, by reference to the mode of payment and demand for rent, not by what the agreement calls it.
  • Expiry of rent does not end the tenancy. A tenancy is determined by a valid notice (or effluxion of a fixed term) — not by the rent running out. A landlord who treats the end of the paid period as the end of the tenancy and moves to eject is acting unlawfully.
  • After the quit notice expires, the landlord serves a further written notice of the owner’s intention to apply to court to recover possession, of not less than seven days. That notice cannot be issued before the quit notice expires.
  • Seven clear days: the day of service is excluded and the day of expiry included.

Only the yearly tier is published. s.6(1)(d) giving a yearly tenant six months is stated by a Rivers practitioner and is consistent with the national pattern. We have not read the primary text, so we do not publish the weekly, monthly or quarterly tiers rather than assume them.

When the notice must expire

This is the Rivers rule that most affects real cases, and it is the opposite of Lagos. s.7: a notice to quit must terminate on the eve of the anniversary of the current term. The notice must end on the day before the next term would renew, and it must cover the full statutory length between the date of service and that eve.

Lagos s.13(4) is the exception: three- and six-month notices there need not end on the anniversary, and may end on or after the term expires. In Rivers — and, on the practitioner account, in most Nigerian states except Lagos — the eve-of-anniversary rule applies. Get the date wrong and the notice is defective.

A yearly tenancy that began on 1 January 2025 runs to 31 December 2025. A valid six-month quit notice must expire on that 31 December — the eve of the anniversary — not a week either side of it.

When rent is due

Rivers reverses the assumption most Nigerian tenants make. s.75(1) of the Landlord and Tenant Law: rent is payable in arrears — at the end of the period — unless the parties agree otherwise.

The national practice is rent in advance, and Lagos s.4 regulates how much advance may be taken. Kwara’s Chapter L4 is the same as Rivers: rent is recoverable only once it is in arrear. In Rivers, unless your agreement says otherwise, paying up front is a concession you are making, not a duty you owe.

Other protections worth knowing

  • A landlord may not resort to self-help — no lock-outs, no removal of goods, no disconnection. Unlawful eviction sounds in damages.
  • A defect in the issue, content or service of a notice vitiates the whole proceeding.
  • The date of service is the material date, not the date written on the notice.

When a notice to quit is valid in Rivers State (Port Harcourt)

  • Correct minimum period — see the table above; a yearly tenant needs six months where the agreement is silent.
  • Proper service — it must reach the tenant in a way you can prove. Service can be on any day and at any hour, but proof is everything.
  • Signed by the right person — the landlord, or an agent/solicitor with written authority dated before the notice is issued.
  • After it expires: a further 7 days’ notice of the owner’s intention to apply to court to recover possession — then the case goes to the Magistrates’ Courts and the High Court of Rivers State.
  • Fixed term already expired? No quit notice is needed at all — only the 7-day court notice.

Questions people ask

Can my landlord give me less than six months’ notice?

If your written agreement specifies a shorter (or longer) notice, that clause generally governs. Where it is silent, the statutory minimums apply.

What if I’m in arrears?

Expiry of rent does not end the tenancy. A tenancy is determined by a valid notice (or effluxion of a fixed term) — not by the rent running out. A landlord who treats the end of the paid period as the end of the tenancy and moves to eject is acting unlawfully.

How do I count the notice period?

Traditionally it had to expire at the end of a complete period of the tenancy — the modern Lagos rule allows expiry on or after the current term ends. When in doubt, give more than the minimum.

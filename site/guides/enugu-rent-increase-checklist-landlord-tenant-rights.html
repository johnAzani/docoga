Enugu landlords: Avoid unlawful rent increases

Mr. Okafor, know your limits when reviewing rent in Enugu.

An increase may not exceed 15% of current rent.

This is law, from s.3(b) of the Landlord and Tenant (Amendment) Law 2008.

You can only review rent after every second anniversary of the tenancy, and it must be by agreement,

not unilateral. An unlawful increase will not be enforced by court.

The law behind it

  • In Enugu a rent increase may not exceed 15% of the existing rent, and rent may be reviewed only after every second anniversary of the tenancy — s.3(b) of the Landlord and Tenant (Amendment) Law 2008, which replaced s.109 of Cap. 101.

Enugu is the one Nigerian state where we have verified a statutory cap on rent increases.
  • A rent increase that ignores the 2008 section — before the second anniversary, above 15%, or imposed without agreement — is an unlawful increment, and a court will not enforce the increased figure.
    Landlord and Tenant Law — A rent increase that ignores the 2008 section — before the second anniversary, above 15%, or imposed without agreement — is an unlawful increment, and a court will not enforce the increased figure.
  • The review is by the parties — a unilateral increase is ineffective.
    Landlord and Tenant (Amendment) Law 2008, s.3(b), replacing s.109 of Cap. 101, Landlord and Tenant Law — Three conditions, all from the section: (1) the review is by the parties — a unilateral increase is ineffective (Jovinco Nigeria Ltd v. Ibeozimako (2014) LPELR-23599(CA): rent increment must be supported by an agreement; Cobra Ltd v. Omole Estate (2000) 1 NWLR (Pt. 655) 1); (2) not before the second anniversary; (3) not more than 15% above the existing rent.

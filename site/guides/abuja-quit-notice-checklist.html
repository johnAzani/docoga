Abuja quit notice checklist: 4 rules your landlord must meet

In Abuja, a yearly tenancy requires half a year (six calendar months) notice to quit.

The full statutory period must fall between service and the stated expiry under the Recovery of Premises Act.

An agent or solicitor needs written authority before issuing a valid notice in Abuja.

Under s.7, a written notice of intention to recover possession giving not less than seven days must follow.

The law behind it

  • In Abuja, a yearly tenancy requires half a year (six calendar months) notice to quit.
    s.8, Recovery of Premises Act — Yearly tenancy: half a year (six calendar months)
  • The full statutory period must fall between service and the stated expiry under the Recovery of Premises Act.
    Recovery of Premises Act — The date of service is what counts, not the date written on the notice. The full statutory period must fall between service and the stated expiry; a month means one calendar month and a year means six calendar months, and no less.
  • An agent or solicitor needs written authority before issuing a valid notice in Abuja.
    Recovery of Premises Act — s.31 attaches the Forms. s.2 defines “agent” as a person usually employed by the landlord in letting or collecting rent, or specially authorised in writing under the landlord’s hand — so an agent or solicitor needs written authority before issuing a valid notice.
  • Under s.7, a written notice of intention to recover possession giving not less than seven days must follow.
    s.7, Recovery of Premises Act — s.7 — once the term has ended or been determined by a written notice to quit in Form B, C or D, the landlord serves written notice in Form E of intention to proceed to recover possession, dated not less than seven days from the date of service. That notice cannot be served before the quit notice expires.

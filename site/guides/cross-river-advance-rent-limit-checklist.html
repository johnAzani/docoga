Did your Cross River landlord demand a full year upfront?

1. Under s.6(6), a landlord cannot demand advance rent exceeding three months from an individual.

2. Demanding or receiving a premium on a tenancy is unlawful under s.6(4).

3. By s.11, the landlord is responsible for structural and external repairs.

4. A yearly tenant is entitled to half a year notice under s.15(1)(d).

The law behind it

  • Under s.6(6), a landlord cannot demand advance rent exceeding three months from an individual.
    s.6(6), Rent Control and Recovery of Premises Law — Cross River is the strictest jurisdiction we have verified — stricter than Lagos. s.6(6): a landlord may demand and receive the prescribed rent in advance for a period not exceeding three months from tenants who are individuals, and twelve months from tenants other than individuals.
  • Demanding or receiving a premium on a tenancy is unlawful under s.6(4).
    s.6(4), Rent Control and Recovery of Premises Law — As in Edo: it is not lawful for anyone, in consideration of the grant, continuance, surrender or giving up of a tenancy, to require or receive any premium. Any such payment is recoverable by the tenant and may be deducted at the time of payment from rent due to the landlord. s.6(5) preserves the bona fide contractor exception.
  • By s.11, the landlord is responsible for structural and external repairs.
    s.11, Rent Control and Recovery of Premises Law — A clean, quotable split: the landlord is responsible for structural and external repairs; the tenant for internal and minor repairs. Compare Lagos, where s.7(3) puts the tenant under a general duty of tenantable repair subject to fair wear and tear.
  • A yearly tenant is entitled to half a year notice under s.15(1)(d).
    s.15(1)(d), Rent Control and Recovery of Premises Law — Yearly tenancy: half a year

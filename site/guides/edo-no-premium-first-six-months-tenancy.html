Edo State: Pay the rent, not the premium

Residential rents are under pressure across Nigeria, but in Edo State, some rules protect you.

Demanding a 'premium' or 'caution fee' beyond rent for a tenancy is unlawful (s.6(4), Edo Edict 1977).

You can deduct any such payment from your next rent.

If you're a new tenant who paid rent in advance for a monthly or quarterly tenancy,

you get a six-month breathing space before a quit notice (s.18 proviso).

The law behind it

  • In Edo State, demanding a 'premium' or 'caution fee' beyond rent for a tenancy is unlawful, according to s.6(4) of the Rent Control and Recovery of Residential Premises Edict 1977.
    s.6(4), Rent Control and Recovery of Residential Premises Edict 1977 — “No premium” is black-letter law in Edo. s.6(4): it is unlawful for anyone, in consideration of the grant, continuance, surrender or giving up of a tenancy, to require or receive any loan or premium. Any such payment is recoverable by the tenant and may, without prejudice to any other method of recovery, be deducted from rent payable to the landlord at any time.
  • If a landlord demands such a payment, you can recover it or deduct it from your next rent.

Any such payment is recoverable by the tenant and may, without prejudice to any other method of recovery, be deducted from rent payable to the landlord at any time.
  • For monthly or quarterly tenancies in Edo, a new tenant who has paid rent in advance has a statutory six-month breathing space before a landlord can give notice to quit, as stated in s.18 proviso of the same Edict.
    s.18 proviso, Rent Control and Recovery of Residential Premises Edict 1977 — Where rent is payable in advance for a monthly or quarterly tenancy, the landlord may not give notice to quit or recover possession during the first six months of the tenancy — except on the order of the tribunal or with the tenant’s consent. A new Edo tenant who has paid in advance has a six-month statutory breathing space.

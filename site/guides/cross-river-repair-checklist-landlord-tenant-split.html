The law behind it

  • Cross River law divides repairs: landlords handle structural and external repairs, tenants handle internal and minor ones.
    s.11, Rent Control and Recovery of Premises Law — A clean, quotable split: the landlord is responsible for structural and external repairs; the tenant for internal and minor repairs. Compare Lagos, where s.7(3) puts the tenant under a general duty of tenantable repair subject to fair wear and tear.
  • In Cross River, a tenant who does improvements with written landlord consent is entitled to compensation at the end.
    s.13, Rent Control and Recovery of Premises Law — A tenant who, with the landlord’s prior consent in writing, executes improvements is entitled to compensation for their unexhausted value at the end of the tenancy. “Improvement” is defined to include structural alteration, extension or addition — including pavements, fences, extra sanitary conveniences and additional fixtures and fittings — but expressly excludes normal repairs.
